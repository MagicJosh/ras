How webhook retries work

A delivery that times out or gets a 5xx is sent again after 30 s, 2 min, 10 min, 1 h and 6 h, then marked failed. A 4xx is never retried, and every try is one row in delivery_attempts. This page is a worked example: the system, its files and its numbers are made up.

The picture

A parcel courier stands for the retry system, pair by pair. The courier and the pairs are made up.

A parcel courierThe retry system
Nobody opens the door, so the courier leaves a card=A timeout or a 5xx is logged as one row
The courier comes back, and waits longer each time=Waits of 30 s, 2 min, 10 min, 1 h, 6 h
After the last visit the parcel goes back to the depot=After 5 retries the delivery is marked failed
Wrong address: coming back will not help=A 4xx is not retried

Where the picture breaks. A courier sees a closed door. The sender sees nothing at all, which is why it needs a timeout to decide that nobody is home.

The pieces

Two tables carry the state, and two short listings carry the rules. The tables, the files and the code are made up.

The attempt log

One row per try in delivery_attempts, with the status and the duration. Count the rows to know how many tries a delivery had.

The due time

next_try_at on the jobs row is the whole schedule. A delivery with no time is not waiting.

Why it is this way

The claim, and the reasons under it. Each link names its evidence. The reasons are made up, and two of them are assumed.

  1. claimRetries wait 30 s, then longer each time, and stop after 5.read: backoff.py:12
  2. becauseA receiver that just failed is most likely still down one second later.assumed
  3. soFive quick retries would all fail, and the delivery would be given up while the receiver is still coming back.follows from the line above
  4. soEach wait is longer than the last. The five waits add up to 7 h 12 min 30 s.computed: 30 + 120 + 600 + 3600 + 21600 s
  5. butA receiver that is down for longer than that needs a person, so the 5th failed retry ends it.assumed

Still open

Assumed or not checked, with what will settle each. All three items are made up.

A receiver that just failed is still down

Assumed, so the first wait is 30 s and not 1 s. Logs of real receivers would show how long failures last.

A long outage needs a person

Assumed, so the delivery is marked failed after the last wait. Asking the receivers how long their outages run settles it.

The worker interval

The spec assumes 10 s, and "Fixed facts" shows it as not checked. Reading the worker's schedule settles it.
